**<u>Rheingold</u>** by Reinhard Herbert

For 3-5 players from 9 years old on up

Rules translation by Walt Mulder 3/18/07

**<u>Background of the Game</u>**

Since the time of Julius Caesar the Rhein has been the center point of
the west lands. A castle on the Rhein commanded influence and power.
Even under the worst conditions, men were drawn to the river to build
the castles. Taxation along the river opened up ripe sources of money
for Robber Barons. This game takes place in a time when many of the
common folk were at the mercy of these Robber Barons. Their never ending
greed for gold and treasure brought together cities and princes to take
action against these robber nests.

The players represent Princes that have called forth Knights from all
points of the compass to band together to fight against the castles. But
often, even the Princes had their own agendas and soon it was one
against the other and every man for himself.

**<u>Game Objective</u>**

The goal of the game is to conquer the most castles. To do this, players
must combine the Knights they bring into the game into strong groups;
for only the strongest groups will have a chance to drive the defenders
from their castles. At the end of the game, the player with the most
valuable (strongest) castles will win. The strength of each castle is
shown by the number of swords next to it.

**<u>Contents</u>**

105 Knights (21 of each color)

1 Knight (12 small figures) – 2 Knights (5 medium figures) – 5 Knights
(4 large figures)

30 Defenders (Shields 6 of each color) worth 0 – 0 – 0 – 1 – 2 – 3

1 Game Board

1 Die

1 Rulebook

**<u>Preparation</u>**

Lay the game board out on the table. The board depicts 18 castles along
the Rhein River with connecting paths and path markers. Also shown are
six starting areas with a coat of arms numbering from 1-6. The swords
next to the castles represent their defensive strength (the number of
swords or the number in the shield from 1-3).

Each player chooses a color and takes all the corresponding Knights and
shields of that color. All the Knights are placed next to the players.
The defense shields are placed face-down in front of the player; the
underside of the shield shows a strength value of 0, 1, 2, or 3 Knights.

Choose a player to start the game. Game play will continue from this
person in clockwise order.

**<u>Game Play</u>**

Summary:

1.  On their turn, each player will roll the die once. One Knight will
    be brought into play on the starting area corresponding to the die
    roll.

2.  The player may now move his Knights a maximum of 3 areas.

3.  During the movement action battles may occur between players.

4.  Battles may also occur when attempting to take possession of a
    castle.

The following are detailed explanations of the various game phases:

**1. Bringing Knights into the Game**

The player whose turn it is rolls the die once. The player then places
one (1) Knight on the starting area (showing 1 to 6 shields)
corresponding to the die roll number.

*Example: A player rolls a 4 on the die. They place one Knight on the
start area showing 4 shields.*

- Start areas are the only locations on the board where Knights from
  different players may be collocated.

- Battles are not allowed on the starting areas.

- Once a Knight leaves a starting area, they may never reenter any
  starting area.

**Important**: You may not place a Knight on a starting area for the
number rolled if you already have a Knight in that area (from a previous
turn).

**2. Moving your Knights**

Movement follows the placing of a Knight in a starting area. Knights may
move along the white dotted lines from the starting area to a connected
path marker space and beyond. The direction of movement may be freely
chosen. Each player receives three moves that they may divide as they
like among their Knights.

*(Depiction 1: shows a move from a start area to an adjoining path
marker.)*

*(Depiction 2: shows two moves along two adjoining path markers.)*

*(Depiction 3: if more that one Knight is in a space, they may all be
moved together to an adjacent path marker and this only counts as one
movement per space moved for the entire group.)*

- Game Tip: Since your Knights are entering the game at scattered
  starting areas, it is an important game element to bring them together
  into stronger groups.

Anytime you have two (2) or five (5) Knight figures together in a space,
you may freely exchange them for a larger corresponding figure (you may
also break them down if necessary).

**3. Battles on Path Marker Spaces**

Only figures of one color may every occupy a single path marker space.
You may not pass through or jump over a path marker occupied by an
opponent’s Knights; they work as a blockade and must be beaten in
battle.

You can only defeat an opponent’s Knights by having at least one (1)
more Knight than they have in a path marker space. For example, one
Knight on a path space may be defeated by two; two knights are defeated
by three, and so on.

Defeated Knights are returned to a player’s stockpile. On their next
turn they will be available again to bring into the game.

**Important**: A player may achieve the required majority to win a
battle by bringing in Knights from multiple directions. However, you may
not exceed the three movement points in order to do so. *(See example in
rules.)*

**4. Conquering an Unoccupied Castle**

All castles start the game with an inherent defense, even if there are
no Knights on their space. The defense strength of a castle is shown by
a number of swords (or the number on the castle shield).

1 Sword = 1 Knight

2 Swords = 2 Knights

3 Swords = 3 Knights

The castle defenders will yield when a player has at least twice the
number of Knights on the path marker leading to the castle **and** at
least one movement point remaining to move into the castle. For example,
this means that a castle with three swords can only be occupied when at
least six Knights are on the space in front of the castle **and** still
have one movement point remaining (*see photo in rulebook*).

**If you have met these requirements, to conquer the castle you need
move only a minimum of one Knight into the castle.** Conquered castles
must always be occupied by a minimum of one Knight. Additional Knights
may be moved into or out of the castle normally so long as one Knight
always remains in the castle.

When a player conquers a castle they **may** immediately place a
defender (the small shields) into the castle. If a player chooses to
place a shield:

- The player mixes up their face-down defender shields.

- One of the shields is drawn randomly. Only the owner of the shield may
  look at the strength on the underside to see its value.

- Place the chosen shield face-down onto the castle space. These
  defenders may never be swapped out or increased with additional
  shields.

Of course you may also conquer castles occupied by other players. That’s
covered next.

**5. Defending your Castles**

Once a castle is in your possession it must be defended by a minimum of
one Knight and (perhaps) a number of defenders. The number of defenders
is known only to the occupying owner (the value under the shield).

**Important**: The numbers of swords next to a castle no longer
determine the strength of the castle. Now only Knights and defenders in
the castle will count.

If you want to conquer a castle occupied by an opponent you must have a
force of Knights that is double the number of defenders in the castle
and occupy the space leading to the castle with at least one movement
point remaining. After declaring the attack, the defender turns over
their shield.

- The shield now remains face up for the remainder of the game (if the
  defender wins).

- If the attacker has at least double the strength of the defender
  (Knights plus shield value) the castle surrenders (all defending
  Knights are returned to the owner’s supply). The defender’s shield is
  removed from the game and the victorious attacker occupies the castle
  as described in 4 above.

- If the attacked has less than twice the number of defenders the battle
  is lost and all the attacker’s Knights are removed and returned to
  their owner’s supply.

*German Rules Example: The castle is defended by 2 Knights and a shield.
It could be a 0, 1, 2, or 3 defense value. If it’s a 3 then the castle’s
total defense value is 5 and would require a minimum of 10 attacking
Knights to be conquered.*

**Important**: The attempt to conquer a castle counts as one movement
point whether successful or not.

**<u>Ending the Game</u>**

The game ends immediately when the next-to-last castle is conquered by a
player. This means that there is one castle remaining that has not been
yet conquered by any player and is still defended by its inherent value.
It doesn’t matter how many times other castles may have changed hands
between players.

Calculate the scores as follows:

- Players score the value of their castles

- Castles are worth as many points as the number swords next to them

- The value of the last unconquered castle is given to the player who
  triggered the end game

- The player with the most castle points wins

- If there is a tie, the player with the most Knights on the board wins

**<u>Unofficial Variants</u>**

These are not part of the rules translation. I would recommend trying
the game first without any changes to the rules. The mechanics in this
game do favor creating larger groups of Knights to be successful in
winning. The random rolls for bringing on Knights tends to even out
among players and those who plan their movements well will be rewarded
and the game truly is excellent the way it is. However, there are some
good suggestions for mitigating the luck of the die or even shortening
the game length. I am posting a few of the best suggestions here that
I’ve found on the web. The can be used individually or in combination.

- **3 Kings Variant**: Each player receives 3 Knights which may be
  brought onto a starting area of their choice in lieu of rolling the
  die (1 per turn of course). This variant allows you to overcome bad
  rolls or to build a large force quicker in a selected are.

- **Quick 6 Variant**: The game ends when one player has placed their
  6<sup>th</sup> shield (note they may not have 6 castles if they lost
  some in battle to opponents). This variant allows for a quicker game
  and perhaps a strategy of grabbing 6 low cost castles fast to end the
  game before other can acquire castles of better values. Also with this
  variant, nobody gets a free castle for triggering the game ending.

- **Pillaging Variant**: When a castle is captured, the entire capturing
  force must enter the castle and cannot move any further that turn.
  This will effectively increase movement required for the capturing
  force as all of them must move in and move out again on the next turn
  thus slowing down larger forces.

- **Attrition Variants**: There are a number of variants out there
  allowing for mild attrition to severe attrition of attacking forces in
  battles. I like the following the best: The defender loses all their
  Knights. The attacker loses as many Knights as the defender (Knights
  only **not** shields). So if you attack a force of 1 with a force of 3
  you lose one Knight. If you attack a force of 2 with 3 Knights, you
  lose two Knights etc. Note that you won’t lose any Knights attacking
  an unoccupied castle as there are no defending “Knights”, and would
  only lose as many Knights as a defender has garrisoning an occupied
  castle. Empty castles would still make for an easy grab but this
  variant could take a bite out of big forces when they attack an enemy
  occupied castle as well as making you think twice before attacking
  small forces with just a +1 advantage.

- **My Shield My Choice Variant**: When placing a shield onto a
  conquered castle you may decide what shield value to place. The value
  still remains hidden until that castle is attacked.
